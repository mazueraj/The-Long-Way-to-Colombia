Critical
🚗 BMW iX2 Check-Up 1 — arrange for ~25 Jun (Week 2)
Ask your friend to swing by around 25 June. The main thing is getting the car running for a bit to keep the 12V battery topped up — this is what keeps the electronics healthy during long storage.

What to do:
1. Sit in the driver's seat, press the brake and press Start — the dashboard lights up (no engine sound, that's normal for an EV).
2. Let it sit in Ready mode with the seatbelt on for 20–30 minutes. A quick spin around the block is even better if they feel like it.
3. Note the battery % on the dashboard and send you a quick message with the number — that's all you need.
4. If the battery is below 30%, plug into the home wallbox before running it.

That's it — shouldn't take more than an hour of their time including the drive over.

⚠️ If the car won't start or shows red warning lights — call BMW Roadside Assist: 1800 813 111 (24/7).
Critical
🚗 BMW iX2 Check-Up 2 — arrange for ~14 Jul (Week 5) + proper drive
This one's the bigger ask — and the more fun one. Around 14 July, ask your friend to take the iX2 out for a proper 30–60 minute drive. Good for the car, and honestly it's a great car to drive so hopefully it's a decent reward for the favour.

What to do:
1. Start it up, let it sit in Ready mode for a few minutes, then take it out.
2. A 30–60 minute drive is ideal — highway, hills, suburbs, whatever they fancy. This exercises the brakes, keeps the tyres in good shape and fully recharges the 12V system.
3. If the battery is below 30% when they start, plug into the home wallbox for a couple of hours before heading out.
4. Park it back inside when done, and send you a message with the battery % from the dashboard and a quick "all good" — that's all you need.

They're welcome to enjoy the drive — no pressure to rush back. Just needs a full licence and to be covered on your insurance as an occasional driver (confirm before you leave).

⚠️ If the car won't start or shows red warning lights — call BMW Roadside Assist: 1800 813 111 (24/7).
Critical
Prepare BMW iX2 for 46-day winter storage — before departure
Based on official BMW manufacturer guidance for long-term EV parking (Autovolt Magazine / BMW ConnectedDrive):

🔋 Charge to 50–70% before leaving — BMW's official guidance states do not park for longer than 14 days if the electric range is critically low. For a 46-day absence, 50–70% is the recommended storage level. Do not leave at 100% or near 0%.

🔌 You can safely leave it connected to your home wallbox — BMW confirms EVs can be left connected as the car stops charging once the battery is full and the BMS manages the rest. If your wallbox supports a charge limit, set it to 70%.

📱 Monitor via BMW ConnectedDrive app — the app shows battery State of Charge remotely. Check every 2–3 weeks. If it drops below 20%, ask your house-checker to plug it in.

⚠️ The 12V auxiliary battery is the real risk for longer absences — BMW iX owner reports confirm the 12V can drain completely over 4–7 weeks, leaving the car dead even if the main battery is fine (the car looks completely dead but the main HV battery still has charge). The BMW iX monitors the 12V and trickle-charges it from the main battery, but this only works if the main battery has enough charge.

🚗 The midway drive (~19 Jul) is important for the 12V — putting the car into 'Ready' mode for 30–60 minutes fully recharges the 12V battery via the DC/DC converter. Ask your friend to do this and confirm they are covered on your insurance before driving.

❄️ Winter storage in QLD is low-risk — Robina winter temperatures are mild (10–22°C). Cold is only problematic below -25°C, so no concern here.

🔕 Disable ConnectedDrive remote features before leaving — constant app polling wakes the car and drains the 12V faster. Set notifications to minimal in the BMW app settings.
